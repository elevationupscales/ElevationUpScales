# Elevation UpScales — Shipping Supplier Qualification Standard

**Status: ACTIVE / CONTROLLING**  
**Effective: 2026-09-08**  
**Owner: Casey Young**  
**Scope: freight carriers, freight forwarders, dangerous-goods providers, receiving warehouses, storage/fulfillment providers, terminals, final-mile providers, Hawaii/Alaska lanes, and other specialized shipping suppliers**

## Purpose

Set one operating standard for qualifying and managing shipping suppliers so Elevation can move difficult products reliably without depending on one carrier, one receiving point, or one undocumented assumption.

This standard supplements the current SOK / Ecommerce / Specialized Shipping SOP and applies to all current and future shipping suppliers.

Core principle:

**EXACT PRODUCT FACTS → COMPLETE PARTNER RESPONSE → ROUTE-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS → VERIFIED ACCEPTANCE → PRICED PROFILE → BOOKING READY → PROVEN SHIPMENT → RECORDED ACTUALS**

Reliability principle:

**NO QUALIFIED CUSTOMER ORDER SHOULD DEPEND ON ONE FREIGHT PROVIDER OR ONE RECEIVING POINT WHEN A PRACTICAL BACKUP CAN BE DEVELOPED.**

---

## 1. Response-completeness gate

Before Elevation sends a follow-up to a shipping supplier, review the full active thread and account for every substantive question, requirement, form request, document request, correction, limitation, quote assumption, or next-step request the supplier has given us.

Every supplier request must end in one of four states:

- **ANSWERED** — Elevation supplied the exact requested fact.
- **SOURCE PENDING** — the fact depends on exact manufacturer, carrier, customer, or shipment data not yet available.
- **BOOKING STAGE** — the information is intentionally withheld until an accepted shipment is ready to book, such as customer PII, final consignee details, payment information, or account forms that are not required for qualification.
- **NOT APPLICABLE / CLOSED** — the supplier confirmed the service is outside its scope or the request no longer applies.

Do not silently skip a supplier question.

Do not ask a supplier to repeat information it has already provided. Acknowledge the earlier answer and build from it.

Do not substitute an estimate when an exact manufacturer or shipment fact is required.

---

## 2. External communication standard

Every shipping-supplier communication should:

1. acknowledge relevant information the supplier has already provided;
2. answer all pending questions Elevation can currently answer;
3. distinguish verified facts from pending facts;
4. use the exact SKU / shipment profile rather than a generic product description when available;
5. identify the exact next decision needed from the supplier;
6. avoid re-asking settled questions;
7. avoid exposing unrelated carrier relationships, private rates, protected routing intelligence, or internal margin mechanics;
8. state limitations explicitly when a manufacturer or warehouse cannot perform a requested preparation step;
9. request route-specific requirements rather than assuming another carrier's requirements apply; and
10. leave the partner with a clear next action.

Professional standard:

**WE SHOULD BE EASIER TO QUOTE, EASIER TO QUALIFY, AND EASIER TO BOOK THAN THE AVERAGE SHIPPER.**

---

## 3. Minimum quote-stage shipment profile

Before requesting a shipment-specific quote, provide every known item that materially affects rating or acceptance.

Minimum profile, as applicable:

- exact manufacturer;
- exact product / SKU;
- commodity description;
- battery chemistry where relevant;
- nominal voltage and watt-hours / kWh where relevant;
- quantity;
- number of pieces / cartons;
- exact packed dimensions per piece;
- exact gross packed weight per piece;
- pallet configuration when relevant;
- pallet dimensions and gross weight when relevant;
- loose-carton vs palletized configuration;
- origin city / ZIP and facility type;
- destination city / ZIP / island;
- terminal / will-call vs door delivery;
- commercial vs residential delivery when relevant;
- declared / merchandise value basis when requested;
- whether goods are new, undamaged, non-defective and not recalled when verified;
- whether SDS/MSDS and UN38.3 are available when relevant;
- receiving / storage / final-mile scope if part of the quote;
- special handling facts known at quote time.

If a carrier prefers its own quote template, answer its template completely rather than forcing Elevation's format onto it.

---

## 4. Lithium / dangerous-goods qualification standard

For standalone lithium batteries and other regulated freight, use the exact-model and exact-route process.

Track:

**FACT → SOURCE → VERIFIED DATE → CARRIER REQUIREMENT → SUPPLIER PREPARATION → CARRIER ACCEPTANCE**

The DG qualification packet may include, depending on the exact carrier and shipment:

- model-specific SDS / MSDS;
- UN38.3 test summary;
- manufacturer/model identification;
- verified chemistry / energy data;
- verified state-of-charge information when required;
- confirmation of new / undamaged / non-defective / non-recalled condition;
- outer-packaging requirements;
- terminal / short-circuit protection;
- separation from conductive materials;
- palletization requirements;
- blocking / bracing / securement requirements;
- stacking limitations;
- required lithium / HazMat marks and labels;
- carrier-issued lithium checklist;
- shipper declaration or other carrier form;
- BOL / booking language;
- shipper / responsible-party signature requirements;
- final packaging / securement photos;
- carrier acceptance evidence.

Never claim a supplier can alter or verify a shipment condition that the supplier has not actually confirmed. For example, a known factory state of charge does not mean an origin warehouse can necessarily re-check or adjust the battery to an arbitrary SOC before shipment.

A generic DG checklist is not carrier acceptance.

**ONE CARRIER'S DG RULES DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY APPLY TO ANOTHER CARRIER.**

---

## 5. Quote, acceptance and booking are separate states

Do not treat these as interchangeable:

- **QUOTED** — a commercial rate was provided for stated assumptions.
- **DG REVIEWED** — the dangerous-goods information was reviewed.
- **ACCEPTED** — the provider confirmed that the exact commodity/profile can move subject to stated requirements.
- **BOOKING READY** — final shipment data, account/onboarding, consignee and booking documents can be supplied and the shipment can be tendered.
- **PROVEN** — at least one actual shipment successfully moved through the route and actuals were recorded.

A rate alone does not prove acceptance.

A prior successful shipment does not prove that a different SKU, quantity, pallet configuration, destination, or carrier service is accepted.

---

## 6. Quote-scope standard

When receiving or requesting a quote, establish what the rate includes and excludes.

As applicable, identify:

- origin transportation;
- origin receiving / handling;
- dangerous-goods / hazmat charges;
- documentation fees;
- palletization / rework;
- terminal fees;
- ocean / linehaul freight;
- destination terminal handling;
- receiving / warehouse charges;
- storage;
- customer release / pick fees;
- loading;
- final-mile delivery;
- residential / liftgate / appointment charges;
- outer-island or remote-area charges;
- declared-value / insurance treatment;
- other accessorials;
- quote validity / expiration;
- transit estimate or sailing assumptions when provided.

Do not assume a prior quote includes a service merely because another quote did.

Do not use one shipment's price for another shipment profile without verification.

---

## 7. Receiving / warehouse supplier standard

For a receiving, storage, fulfillment, will-call, or final-mile partner, qualify at least:

- whether the exact commodity can be received and stored;
- new-vs-used product restrictions;
- inbound appointment / advance-notice procedure;
- required consignee / reference / BOL information;
- documents required before arrival;
- pallet size / weight / storage-space limitations;
- receiving fees and how space is measured;
- storage method and billing basis;
- customer / installer release authorization procedure;
- outbound / loading scope;
- local delivery capability;
- inter-island / remote-area capability when relevant;
- quote inputs required for each delivery;
- visible-damage documentation procedure;
- concealed-damage / loss / claims procedure;
- return / damaged-product isolation procedure;
- inventory-count / reconciliation method when inventory is held.

Keep the roles distinct:

**FREIGHT PROVIDER ≠ RECEIVING WAREHOUSE ≠ RETAILER / RESELLER**

A company may perform more than one role, but each role must be separately understood and commercially scoped.

---

## 8. Booking-stage information boundary

Do not expose protected information earlier than required.

Booking-stage information may include:

- final customer / consignee name;
- phone and email;
- exact delivery address;
- account applications;
- credit or payment forms;
- bank or card information;
- internal PO information;
- exact shipment value when required;
- final shipper identity;
- final pickup / delivery appointment details.

Share protected information only with the party that needs it for the active shipment and only when it is operationally necessary.

Never place private payment data, customer PII, private rates, account forms containing sensitive information, or protected commercial terms in the public repository.

---

## 9. Supplier qualification states

Use the following operational states:

**DISCOVERED**  
**→ CONTACTED**  
**→ REQUIREMENTS RECEIVED**  
**→ ELEVATION QUESTIONS ANSWERED**  
**→ EXACT SKU PROFILE SENT**  
**→ DG / COMMODITY REVIEW**  
**→ PRICED**  
**→ ACCEPTED**  
**→ BOOKING READY**  
**→ PROVEN**  
**→ PROFILE VERIFIED / REVALIDATE**

A supplier may also be classified by role:

- **PRIMARY ROUTE**
- **SECONDARY ROUTE**
- **BACKUP ROUTE**
- **FUTURE LARGE-LOAD ONLY**
- **RECEIVING ONLY**
- **FINAL-MILE ONLY**
- **WAREHOUSING / FULFILLMENT**
- **NOT QUALIFIED**

Do not call a route "qualified" merely because an introductory email was sent or a general capability statement was received.

---

## 10. Redundancy standard

For strategic Hawaii / Alaska and dangerous-goods lanes, build practical redundancy.

Preferred architecture:

**PRIMARY FREIGHT ROUTE**  
**+ SECONDARY / BACKUP FREIGHT ROUTE(S)**  
**+ PRIMARY RECEIVING OPTION**  
**+ BACKUP RECEIVING OPTION WHEN PRACTICAL**

A backup route must eventually be qualified using the same standard as the primary route. It is not a real backup until the commodity, route requirements and commercial profile are understood well enough to use it.

Do not expose the complete route network to any outside partner unless operationally necessary.

---

## 11. Pending-question audit before every send

Before sending a freight / warehouse / shipping-supplier email, perform this check:

- Read the active thread.
- Identify every partner question/request.
- Verify that every available answer is included.
- Mark facts still dependent on manufacturer/customer/exact route.
- Carry forward supplier-provided assumptions and limitations.
- Confirm that we are not asking a settled question again.
- Verify SKU/dimensions/weights against the current source.
- Verify DG claims against the current manufacturer source.
- Verify destination/service type.
- Ask only the unresolved questions needed to advance qualification.
- State the next operational action after the supplier responds.

**NO PARTNER QUESTION SHOULD DISAPPEAR BETWEEN EMAILS.**

---

## 12. Revalidation standard

Revalidate a route when:

- a quote expires or becomes stale;
- a carrier changes requirements;
- a new SKU is introduced;
- quantity or packaging materially changes;
- shipment mode changes;
- origin or destination changes;
- terminal vs delivery changes;
- palletization changes;
- DG documents change;
- warehouse or receiving requirements change;
- a provider states that prior pricing or acceptance no longer applies.

Do not promise a fixed freight rate, sailing, transit time or delivery date from stale information.

---

## 13. Actuals after shipment

Every completed specialized shipment should reduce rediscovery on the next order.

Record, in a protected operational system where appropriate:

- exact SKU / quantity;
- final packing configuration;
- actual origin handoff;
- actual carrier / route;
- final DG documents used;
- marks / labels used;
- booking issues;
- actual freight and accessorials;
- receiving/storage charges;
- transit time;
- damage / exception events;
- final delivery or pickup method;
- owner/manager time;
- lessons / changed requirements;
- revalidation date.

Then update the route profile for the next shipment.

**MANUAL FIRST → ACTUALS → REPEATABLE PROFILE → SOP → AUTOMATION**

---

## 14. Public / private boundary

This repository is public. Keep this standard public-safe.

Do not commit:

- carrier buy-rates;
- quote numbers when they expose private commercial information;
- private partner identities when not operationally necessary;
- private negotiated rate cards;
- raw supplier/dealer costs;
- customer PII;
- account applications containing protected data;
- card/bank/payment information;
- private DG correspondence or non-public compliance packets;
- internal margin mechanics;
- fallback-route identities when disclosure would expose protected network intelligence.

Public operations files may record the process, status categories and decision rules without revealing the protected commercial network.

---

## 15. Operating standard

Elevation should approach every shipping supplier as an organized commercial shipper, not as a customer asking a carrier to invent the shipment for us.

**ANSWER THEIR QUESTIONS.**  
**GIVE EXACT PRODUCT FACTS.**  
**ASK FOR THEIR EXACT ROUTE RULES.**  
**SEPARATE QUOTE FROM ACCEPTANCE.**  
**DOCUMENT THE SCOPE.**  
**PROTECT THE NETWORK.**  
**BUILD A BACKUP.**  
**RECORD ACTUALS.**  
**MAKE THE NEXT SHIPMENT EASIER.**
