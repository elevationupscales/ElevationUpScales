# Elevation UpScales — Hawaii Retail Channel Partner SOP

**Status: ACTIVE / CONTROLLING**  
**Effective: 2026-09-08**  
**Updated: 2026-09-09**  
**Owner: Casey Young**  
**Scope: Hawaii retail/reseller accounts, local fulfillment nodes, SOK coordination, vendor pricing, relationship protection**

## Purpose

Establish the operating structure for a Hawaii retail partner that may store and handle Elevation-owned low-voltage inventory, support local customer pickup or walk-in demand, purchase selected inventory from Elevation for resale, and help qualify existing battery-forwarder capacity.

This SOP supplements `MANAGEMENT_OPERATING_SOP.md`, `LOGISTICS_PRICING_MODEL.md`, `SOK_ECOMMERCE_SHIPPING_SOP.md`, and `SOK_HAWAII_FIRST_ORDER_READINESS.md`.

The controlling commercial model is:

**SOK / MANUFACTURER SUPPLY → ELEVATION CONTROLS THE COMMERCIAL RELATIONSHIP + LOGISTICS → HAWAII LOCAL NODE → ONLINE / WALK-IN / B2B CUSTOMER → REORDER**

The goal is not merely to rent storage. The goal is to create a repeatable Hawaii sales, fulfillment, referral, and replenishment channel while protecting the relationships and operating assets Elevation creates.

---

## 1. Current strategic direction

A qualified Hawaii retail/fulfillment partner may create four connected capabilities:

1. local receiving, storage, handling, and customer release for Elevation-owned inventory;
2. a local walk-in sales channel using Elevation inventory under an approved commercial structure;
3. a repeat B2B account that may purchase selected products from Elevation at approved vendor pricing;
4. access to an existing battery-forwarder path that may be tested with an exact SOK low-voltage shipment.

Do not treat the partner as only a warehouse. Evaluate the relationship as a potential:

**FULFILLMENT NODE + LOCAL SALES CHANNEL + REPEAT B2B CUSTOMER + FREIGHT-ACCESS PARTNER.**

Do not interfere with a partner's established 48V / high-voltage supplier relationships where the partner has stated those lines are protected. Build first around low-voltage consumer, RV, smaller off-grid, backup-power, charging, monitoring, protection, accessories, and other complementary products.

## 2. Approved commercial structures

### Model A — Elevation-owned inventory / local fulfillment

Elevation owns the inventory and remains seller of record.

Flow:

**CUSTOMER → ELEVATION SALE → LOCAL PARTNER RELEASE / PICKUP / HANDLING**

The local partner may receive approved handling, storage, pickup, release, or other service compensation under the agreed commercial terms.

This model does not require the local partner to buy the product from Elevation.

### Model B — Downstream retailer / reseller account

The local partner purchases product from Elevation at an approved vendor/reseller price and resells it to its own customer.

Flow:

**SOK / SUPPLIER → ELEVATION → LOCAL RETAILER → END CUSTOMER**

For SOK products, the downstream-resale boundary is now verified:

- Elevation may wholesale SOK products to a downstream Hawaii retailer/reseller;
- the downstream retailer may purchase through Elevation under Elevation's existing dealer relationship without separate SOK approval for this structure;
- the downstream retailer's advertised/end-customer pricing must remain at or above SOK MAP;
- the preferred support path is **RETAILER → ELEVATION → SOK** for warranty/RMA handling.

Do not price a legitimate retail/reseller account at ordinary public retail merely because MAP controls advertised end-customer pricing.

### Model C — Hybrid local node — preferred development path

Elevation maintains a limited amount of Elevation-owned inventory at the local partner for ecommerce fulfillment and local pickup, while the partner may also purchase selected units from Elevation at approved vendor pricing when it has its own walk-in or business demand.

Flow:

**ONE LOCAL INVENTORY POSITION → ELEVATION ONLINE SALES + APPROVED LOCAL RETAIL PURCHASES → REPLENISHMENT**

Use inventory controls that clearly distinguish Elevation-owned stock from inventory sold to or owned by the local partner.

SOK has confirmed that appropriate Hawaii customer/dealer referrals may still route to Elevation when a local fulfillment/retail partner holds inventory, with Elevation controlling the downstream sale and partner arrangement.

## 3. Vendor pricing lane

The Hawaii downstream retailer/reseller model is a distinct commercial lane from ordinary Elevation retail and from standalone third-party logistics facilitation.

Vendor pricing must be built from current verified economics, not from an arbitrary discount off retail.

Internal pricing inputs may include:

- current supplier/dealer cost basis;
- exact SKU and quantity;
- origin preparation cost when applicable;
- current specialized freight and DG/hazmat cost;
- Hawaii receiving / storage / handling cost;
- payment-processing and transaction cost;
- Elevation required contribution/margin;
- quantity efficiency and replenishment economics;
- applicable MAP / advertised retail constraints.

Recommended commercial tiers may include:

- sample / proof quantity;
- small local restock;
- dealer stocking quantity;
- larger commercial replenishment.

SOK has now confirmed two supplier-side quantity lanes for warehouse pickup: a current no-MOQ structure for smaller quantities and the normal warehouse wholesale structure for qualifying mixed/commercial quantities. Exact supplier costs and breakpoints remain protected commercial information and must not be placed in this public repository.

Exact breakpoints, vendor prices, supplier costs, margins, freight buy-rates, and partner fees belong in a protected internal rate card or quote model.

The retailer-facing price sheet should normally contain only public-safe commercial fields such as:

- SKU / product;
- approved vendor price;
- applicable MAP or retail reference where appropriate;
- quantity tier;
- availability / replenishment status;
- high-level Hawaii fulfillment terms.

Do not expose supplier cost, carrier buy-rate, internal logistics margin, forwarder identity, or protected route intelligence.

## 4. Verified SOK commercial boundary

The following SOK points are now verified and do not need to be re-asked in routine partner development:

1. **Downstream resale:** Elevation may wholesale SOK products to a Hawaii retailer/reseller.
2. **Retailer approval:** the downstream retailer may buy through Elevation under Elevation's dealer relationship; separate SOK approval is not required for this structure.
3. **MAP:** downstream advertised/end-customer pricing must remain at or above SOK MAP.
4. **Warranty/RMA relationship path:** SOK prefers **RETAILER → ELEVATION → SOK**.
5. **Hawaii warranty fulfillment proposal:** because SOK does not directly ship replacements to Hawaii, SOK proposed including replacement parts/units with a later Elevation order where practical and offsetting the related shipping impact. This is a supplier proposal and must receive owner acceptance before becoming Elevation's standard warranty operating method.
6. **Small-quantity pickup:** SOK has a current no-MOQ warehouse-pickup structure for quantities below its normal wholesale threshold.
7. **Commercial replenishment:** qualifying mixed/commercial quantities may use SOK's normal warehouse wholesale structure.
8. **Referral control:** appropriate Hawaii customer/dealer referrals continue to come to Elevation even if a local partner holds inventory; Elevation controls the downstream sale and partner arrangement.
9. **One-unit proof release:** SOK has confirmed it can release 1 × SK12V100PC from Chino for a qualification movement under the current small-quantity pickup structure.

These confirmations allow Elevation to move from "waiting on SOK permission" to **building the actual landed-cost and local-partner commercial model**.

Do not request exclusivity as a condition of the first proof shipment.

The near-term objective is to establish Elevation operationally as a reliable Hawaii channel for suitable low-voltage / difficult-freight SOK demand, then use actual performance and volume to support any later discussion of stronger territory or channel protections.

## 6. Relationship and commercial-asset protection

Before Elevation shares increasingly sensitive supplier, customer, pricing, route, referral, or channel information with a commercial partner, use an appropriate written agreement for the actual relationship.

Preferred protections include:

- confidentiality / NDA obligations;
- non-circumvention of specifically introduced relationships and opportunities;
- introduced-account / referral protection;
- permitted use of supplier, customer, pricing, and logistics information;
- ownership of customer and transaction data;
- inventory ownership and risk-of-loss rules;
- receiving, storage, handling, release, and reconciliation responsibilities;
- reseller/vendor pricing and payment terms;
- MAP / brand-use obligations where applicable;
- warranty, return, and RMA responsibility;
- settlement and inventory-reconciliation process;
- termination and treatment of remaining inventory;
- reasonable post-termination protection for specifically introduced accounts, suppliers, or opportunities where legally appropriate.

Do not default to a broad market-wide non-compete merely to protect Elevation's work. Use targeted confidentiality, non-circumvention, introduced-account, and commercial-contract protections first. Any restrictive covenant, exclusivity provision, territory restriction, or non-compete language should receive qualified legal review for the applicable parties and jurisdictions before execution.

No SOP language itself creates a binding contract with a supplier, retailer, customer, freight provider, or other partner.

## 9. Warranty / RMA control for the Hawaii retailer lane

The confirmed relationship path is:

**END CUSTOMER → LOCAL RETAILER → ELEVATION → SOK**

Until Casey approves a standing Hawaii warranty execution method, each actual replacement/return should still be reviewed for:

- exact failure / warranty eligibility;
- whether a replacement unit, component, refund, or other remedy is authorized by SOK;
- safe and lawful handling of any damaged/defective lithium battery;
- whether the replacement should move with an Elevation replenishment order or another qualified route;
- customer timing and communication;
- who bears any documented incremental freight or handling cost under the approved transaction.

Do not assume a defective lithium battery can be returned through the same route as new product.
