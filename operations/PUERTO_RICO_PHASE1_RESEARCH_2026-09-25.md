## 1. Market signal — strong and measurable

The Puerto Rico battery-storage market is not merely a hurricane-event hypothesis.

U.S. EIA reported that at the end of 2025 Puerto Rico had:

- 191,929 rooftop solar systems;
- 171,372 distributed battery-storage systems;
- 2,864 MWh of distributed battery-storage energy capacity;
- an average of about 3,850 new rooftop solar systems installed per month during 2025.

PREB/LUMA filings continue to show active distributed-generation/interconnection work through Q2 2026. Separate utility-scale storage development is also substantial.

**Phase 1 interpretation:** Elevation would be entering an established, high-adoption storage market. The opportunity is not creating awareness of batteries; it is competing on product, availability, landed economics, logistics, warranty support and customer execution.

## 5. Puerto Rico IVU / destination release

Puerto Rico Treasury states:

- IVU includes both sales tax and use tax;
- taxable property introduced into Puerto Rico is subject to IVU;
- for maritime imports, IVU is paid before release;
- this includes property imported for use and property imported for resale/inventory;
- import declarations and related IVU processes are electronic through SURI;
- importers must declare imported merchandise even when an exemption may apply.

Marketplace-facilitator rules are distinct from direct-site sales. Puerto Rico Treasury states marketplace facilitators collect IVU on transactions through their marketplace. Direct-site nexus/registration and the exact importer-of-record/release structure still require a transaction-specific qualified determination before launch.

Sources:
- https://hacienda.pr.gov/node/3107
- https://hacienda.pr.gov/node/1818
- https://hacienda.pr.gov/ivu/registro-de-comerciante
- https://hacienda.pr.gov/node/4694

**Phase 1 control:** do not advertise "tax included," "tax exempt," or a customer-paid-at-terminal tax model until the planned Elevation transaction structure is resolved.

## 6. EEI / AES

Census/Trade.gov confirm that shipments from the United States to Puerto Rico are within EEI/AES reporting rules.

General threshold:

- EEI is required when value is over **$2,500 per Schedule B number**, or when another mandatory filing rule applies;
- the low-value exemption can apply at $2,500 or less per Schedule B when no mandatory filing requirement applies;
- U.S.–Puerto Rico shipments require the filing/exemption citation to be provided to the carrier under the Puerto Rico filing rules.

Sources:
- https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/regulations/regs/flipper/
- https://www.trade.gov/electronic-export-information-eei
- https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/trade/guide/index_flipper_redesign.html
- https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/schedules/b/index.html

Planning implication:

- 3 × SK12V100PC at current MAP remains below it;

- 3 × SK48V100N at current MAP exceeds $2,500 for the same product classification and is therefore an **EEI-likely case**, subject to final Schedule B/value/mandatory-filing determination.

Do not split a commercial shipment to avoid EEI.

## 7. Phase 1 working conclusion

The research now supports moving Puerto Rico forward as a **real qualification lane**, not merely a speculative expansion idea.

The strongest evidence is:

1. very large installed distributed battery base;
2. continuing solar/storage adoption;
3. established local 48V battery competition;
4. current SOK supplier economics and exact packed profiles already available;
5. at least one major Puerto Rico ocean carrier has a formal HAZMAT review path;
6. the tax/import-release and EEI processes are identifiable enough to build the exact qualification checklist.
