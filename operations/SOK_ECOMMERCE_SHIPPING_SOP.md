# Elevation UpScales — SOK / Ecommerce / Specialized Shipping SOP

**Status: ACTIVE / CONTROLLING**  
**Version: 2.1 — 2026-09-07**  
**Owner: Casey Young**

## Purpose

Operate Elevation UpScales as one ecommerce system specializing in lithium energy, SOK batteries, RV/outdoor equipment, battery accessories and chargers, off-grid products, and difficult Hawaii/Alaska fulfillment.

The original RV / Outdoor Store is the ecommerce baseline from which the Lithium Store has grown. These are not separate businesses. They are customer-facing lanes inside one Elevation UpScales commerce and logistics system.

Core principle:

**CUSTOMER BUYS → ELEVATION CONTROLS THE TRANSACTION → ELEVATION SOLVES THE LOGISTICS → SUPPLIER RELEASES PRODUCT → FREIGHT MOVES → CUSTOMER RECEIVES ORDER**

Do not reject good demand merely because fulfillment requires manual work.

---

## 1. Customer fulfillment lanes

### Lane A — Lower 48
Normal ecommerce.

### Lane B — Hawaii
Elevation-managed specialized shipping, freight, hazmat coordination and logistics.

### Lane C — Alaska
Elevation-managed specialized shipping and logistics.

The same product should use these destination lanes wherever operationally supported. The shipping method changes; the shopping experience should remain simple.

## 2. Lower-48 ecommerce rule — entire store

Lower-48 simplicity applies across:

- Lithium Store
- SOK products
- RV Store
- Outdoor Store
- lithium accessories
- battery chargers
- solar/off-grid accessories
- other normally shippable Elevation products

Desired flow:

**PRODUCT → QUANTITY → SHIPPING ADDRESS → PAYMENT → CONFIRMATION**

Do not make normal Lower-48 buyers complete freight or project qualification when the product is eligible for ordinary fulfillment.

Do not ask them to understand:

- freight carriers
- hazmat preparation
- pallets
- dangerous-goods paperwork
- supplier warehouses
- supplier POs
- carrier qualification
- internal routing

Those are Elevation responsibilities.

For SOK Lower-48 orders, the operating flow is:

**CUSTOMER PAYS → ELEVATION VALIDATES → ELEVATION PO → SOK ORDER PROCESS → SHIPMENT → TRACKING → CUSTOMER**

For RV/outdoor Lower-48 orders, use the same normal-ecommerce principle whenever parcel or established freight fulfillment exists.

**MAKE LOWER-48 SHOPPING FEEL LIKE NORMAL ECOMMERCE.**

## 3. Paid-order recovery rule

For SOK batteries:

**A SUCCESSFULLY PAID ORDER BECOMES AN ELEVATION FULFILLMENT RESPONSIBILITY.**

An internal system failure does not automatically cancel the order.

Failures involving the website, Shopify, APIs, databases, email automation, inventory synchronization, PO generation, routing logic or carrier integrations trigger:

**MANUAL ORDER RECOVERY**

when the order can still reasonably and lawfully be fulfilled.

Minimum recoverable order information:

- proof of successful payment
- customer name
- customer phone
- customer email
- shipping address
- exact SKU/product
- quantity

Then continue manually:

**ORDER → ELEVATION PO / SUPPLIER ORDER → PROCUREMENT → SHIPPING ROUTE → TRACKING → CUSTOMER**

Automation assists fulfillment. Automation failure does not define whether Elevation can fulfill the order.

A genuine legal, safety, fraud, payment, product-availability or transportation impossibility may still require hold/refund handling.

## 4. RV / Outdoor shipping rule

The RV / Outdoor Store follows the same Lower-48 simplicity standard as Lithium.

Hawaii and Alaska shipping options for RV/outdoor products may still be under development product-by-product. That does not reduce Lower-48 availability.

A product may therefore be:

- normal Lower-48 checkout
- Hawaii specialized-shipping eligible
- Alaska specialized-shipping eligible
- Hawaii/Alaska route under review

Do not remove a valid Lower-48 product merely because Hawaii logistics for that item are still being developed.

Hawaii is not simply a checkout exception. It is a strategic Elevation logistics lane.

Elevation develops the operating bridge between:

**SUPPLIER / SOK ORIGIN**

**→ CARRIER / FORWARDER**  
**→ HAWAII RECEIVING**  
**→ CUSTOMER / DEALER**

For SOK Hawaii orders, Elevation's operational job is to facilitate the order and determine what the SOK origin/hazmat team must prepare for the selected Elevation-arranged transportation path.

That may include verified requirements for:

- marks and labels
- dangerous-goods documentation
- SDS / UN38.3 support
- carrier-specific forms

- appointments

Do not assume two carriers have identical requirements.

Public partnership titles must still use the exact currently approved SOK relationship wording. Do not create unsupported exclusivity claims.

A Hawaii customer who wants an eligible product should have a purchase path.

Do not default to:

**Unavailable — Contact Us**

when Elevation can reasonably support a paid-order or prepurchase path.

Appropriate states may include:

- Buy Now — Hawaii Freight
- Hawaii Freight & Logistics
- Prepurchase
- Special Order
- Freight Review

If a product is out of stock, awaiting replenishment or not yet released, prepurchase terms may be used only when the supplier facts support accepting the order.

Prepurchase terms and timelines must be product-specific and current. Do not use one generic availability promise across the catalog.

## 9. Pre-payment specialized-shipping disclosure

Before the customer pays, present a short plain-English disclosure substantially equivalent to:

> Hawaii and Alaska orders may require specialized freight coordination. Elevation UpScales manages the shipping and logistics process for your order. Delivery timing and final freight costs may vary based on product availability, weather, carrier availability, destination, shipment size, hazardous-material requirements and actual transportation conditions. The shipping amount collected at checkout may be estimated or preliminary, and the customer remains responsible for the reasonable documented final shipping cost required to complete delivery under the accepted purchase agreement.

The buyer must affirmatively accept the applicable specialized-shipping agreement before payment.

The goal is informed consent, not freight education.

Do not expose private carrier negotiations, supplier costs or internal hazmat procedures.

## 10. Hawaii / Alaska purchase and freight agreement

The customer agreement must remain readable to an ordinary ecommerce customer and must establish at least the following.

### 10.1 Product purchase
The customer is purchasing the identified product(s) from Elevation UpScales.

### 10.2 Specialized shipping
The order may require specialized freight, parcel handling, dangerous-goods preparation, forwarding, terminal handling, receiving or final-mile delivery.

### 10.3 Elevation coordinates the shipment
Elevation may work with qualified carriers, freight forwarders, terminals, receiving providers, suppliers and other transportation resources to complete the order.

The customer is not expected to coordinate the supplier with the freight provider.

### 10.4 Multiple routing options
Elevation may use a primary route, alternate carrier, forwarder, terminal, consolidator, receiving partner or final-mile provider when necessary to complete delivery.

### 10.5 Initial freight may not be final freight
The amount shown during checkout may be confirmed, estimated or preliminary based on the best available route at the time of purchase.

Actual final freight may change because of shipment-specific conditions.

### 10.6 Customer responsibility for final freight
The customer agrees to pay the reasonable documented final shipping costs required to complete the order, including applicable freight, hazardous-material handling, terminal/accessorial charges, final-mile charges and separately disclosed Elevation shipping/coordination charges that apply to the actual order.

Do not add arbitrary or undisclosed charges.

### 10.7 Final shipping reconciliation
Actual freight cost may not be fully known until the shipment is transported and delivered.

Elevation may reconcile actual documented freight against the amount previously collected.

### 10.8 Final balance after delivery
If documented final freight exceeds the amount already collected, the customer remains responsible for the remaining balance and may receive a final shipping reconciliation charge after delivery.

### 10.9 Payment authorization
The customer must expressly authorize Elevation to collect a documented final shipping balance using the payment method associated with the order **only where the payment processor/platform supports a compliant subsequent charge based on that authorization**.

If the processor/platform does not support that method, Elevation must issue a final invoice or payment request instead of making an unsupported card charge.

### 10.10 Timing
Shipping timelines may be affected by weather, product supply, carrier schedules, destination, quantity, shipment size, terminal conditions, dangerous-goods requirements, carrier acceptance and supplier preparation.

Delivery dates are estimates unless explicitly guaranteed by the responsible transportation provider.

### 10.11 Route failure
If the planned shipping option becomes unavailable:

**CARRIER FAILURE → RE-ROUTE**

not automatically:

**CARRIER FAILURE → CANCEL ORDER**

Elevation should evaluate commercially reasonable alternate routes first.

### 10.12 Customer cooperation
The customer must provide accurate contact, destination and receiving information and cooperate with reasonable delivery requirements.

### 10.13 Safety / carrier acceptance
All shipments remain subject to applicable law, dangerous-goods requirements, carrier acceptance and actual transportation feasibility.

Customer-facing legal/payment language should receive final legal/payment-provider review before production implementation, particularly any post-delivery payment authorization.

Do not design Hawaii fulfillment around one transportation provider.

**PRIMARY ROUTE → SECONDARY ROUTE → BACKUP ROUTE**

If one route declines, becomes unavailable or becomes uneconomical, evaluate another qualified route before declaring the order impossible.

## 17. Logistics asset-protection and disclosure boundary

Elevation's working logistics network, route knowledge and fulfillment methods are commercial assets.

The system may use that information internally to execute orders, but information must be disclosed on a **minimum-necessary basis**.

### 17.1 Internal deployment / authorized admin may know

Authorized internal systems and managers may use protected operational details when necessary to execute or troubleshoot a shipment, including:

- exact working carrier / forwarder;
- primary, secondary and backup route identity;
- exact route-specific acceptance requirements;
- carrier-issued lithium / HazMat checklists;
- route-specific state-of-charge or capacity review rules;
- booking / quote reference;
- origin terminal and handoff instructions;
- receiving / storage / final-mile partner identity;
- carrier-specific labels / documentation instructions;
- route timing, cutoff, consolidation and sailing intelligence;
- internal route-selection logic;
- private carrier pricing and accessorial structures;
- internal margin / coordination economics;
- protected shipment evidence and carrier correspondence.

This information belongs behind an authorized operational boundary. It must not be exposed merely because the application has access to it.

### 17.2 Customer invoices / receipts — strict minimum disclosure

Invoices, receipts, payment requests and freight-reconciliation invoices should normally show only what the customer needs to understand and pay the transaction, such as:

- Elevation order / invoice number;
- customer name and billing/shipping information as required;
- product / SKU or customer-appropriate product description;
- quantity;
- retail product price;
- customer-facing shipping / freight charge;
- separately disclosed Elevation shipping / handling / logistics coordination charge when applicable;
- taxes / authorized fees;
- amount paid / balance due;
- high-level shipping status or tracking information when appropriate;
- plain-English note that specialized freight may involve qualified third-party transportation providers.

Invoices and receipts must **not** expose, unless legally or operationally required for that exact transaction:

- freight carrier / forwarder identity;
- backup carrier or alternate route identity;
- raw carrier buy rate;
- quote number or booking reference;
- freight markup / internal margin;
- terminal addresses used only as internal handoff points;
- receiving / storage partner identity;
- private final-mile partner identity;
- carrier-specific HazMat checklist details;
- exact carrier acceptance thresholds;
- dangerous-goods preparation playbook;
- internal labels / paperwork instructions;
- supplier warehouse process beyond what the customer must know;
- supplier/dealer cost;
- internal PO / payment terms;
- route-selection logic;
- internal operational notes;
- private carrier or supplier correspondence.

Customer-facing documents should sell and document **Elevation's product + shipping/logistics service**, not reveal how to reconstruct Elevation's logistics network.

### 17.3 Customer-facing website / email / support disclosure

Customer communications may explain:

- that specialized freight is required;
- that Elevation coordinates qualified transportation resources;
- the customer-facing freight amount or estimate;
- pickup vs delivery method;
- timing/status;
- required customer actions;
- tracking / release instructions when necessary;
- final freight reconciliation.

Do not volunteer route architecture, carrier comparison, internal carrier requirements, private rates, supplier-side preparation methods or the identity of unnecessary intermediaries.

### 17.4 Supplier / SOK disclosure — origin preparation only

SOK or another supplier should receive only the information necessary to prepare and release the specific shipment, including as applicable:

- exact model / SKU and quantity;
- required manufacturer documents for that exact model;
- battery condition requirements;
- state-of-charge requirement when applicable to the selected shipment;
- physical packaging / securement requirements the supplier is being asked to perform;
- required origin-side marks / labels;
- required shipper-side forms / certifications;
- required preparation photos / evidence;
- the specific origin handoff destination or booking instruction when SOK must physically tender the freight there.

Do not disclose to SOK merely for background or convenience:

- Elevation's complete carrier network;
- alternate / backup carriers;
- private carrier rates or quote comparisons;
- internal margin;
- receiving-network strategy;
- unrelated Hawaii warehouse / delivery partners;
- route-selection logic;
- other customers' shipment information;
- broader protected logistics intelligence not required for SOK to perform the shipment.

### 17.5 Freight carrier / receiving-provider disclosure

A carrier, forwarder, terminal, warehouse or receiving provider may receive the shipment data necessary to quote, accept, move, store, release or deliver the exact freight, including required DG/product/customer information.

Do not provide unrelated supplier commercial terms, other carrier quotes, backup route intelligence, internal margin or broader customer/business records.

### 17.6 Public repository boundary

Never expose through public Git, storefront, public documentation or unrestricted deployment artifacts without explicit approval:

- supplier/dealer cost;
- internal margin;
- raw supplier inventory;
- private supplier correspondence;
- private supplier payment terms;
- private carrier identities tied to Elevation's working route network when commercially sensitive;
- private carrier quotes / rates;
- quote / booking references;
- exact carrier-specific thresholds or proprietary route playbooks;
- origin terminal / protected handoff network;
- private receiving / storage partner identities and terms;
- backup carrier identities;
- internal route-comparison / selection logic;
- internal HazMat negotiations;
- carrier-issued non-public checklists / compliance packets;
- customer personal data;
- protected shipment evidence.

Public Git may retain generic workflow states and data-field requirements necessary to build the system without publishing the underlying route intelligence.

### 17.7 Disclosure test

Before information leaves the protected operational boundary, ask:

1. **Does this recipient need this exact detail to perform their role?**
2. **Would removing this detail prevent the transaction or shipment from being completed?**
3. **Could this detail help an outside party reconstruct Elevation's supplier, carrier, receiving or pricing network?**

If the recipient does not need the detail, do not disclose it.

Default rule:

**INTERNAL SYSTEM KNOWS → EXTERNAL PARTY RECEIVES ONLY WHAT IT NEEDS.**

## 18. AI / worker decision rule

When a worker encounters a shipping complication, first determine:

**CAN ELEVATION SOLVE THIS MANUALLY OR THROUGH ANOTHER VERIFIED SHIPPING ROUTE?**

before concluding:

**WE CANNOT TAKE / COMPLETE THE ORDER.**

Default behavior:

**CAPTURE DEMAND → TAKE VALID ORDERS → RECOVER MANUALLY WHEN NECESSARY → FIND A ROUTE → COMPLETE THE ORDER**

Hard stops are reserved for genuine legal, safety, fraud, payment, actual product-availability or transportation-impossibility conditions.

Operational inconvenience alone is not a sufficient reason to abandon a customer.
