## Executive finding

eBay has a **demand problem only on some listings; the larger problem is economics + fulfillment control**.

The store has proven that customers will buy camping cots/folding beds, boot dryers, a spotlight, lawn sweeper, organizer, lithium batteries and other utility products. However, several sales were priced below the source structure needed to produce acceptable contribution, and late/unresolved fulfillment has tied up cash and created cancellations/refunds.

The correct recovery is therefore **not a catalog-growth campaign and not a total reset**.

It is:

**RECOVER CASH → CONTRACT CATALOG → PRESERVE PROVEN DEMAND → REMOVE BAD ECONOMICS → REBUILD SOURCE/PRICE → FULFILL IMMEDIATELY → RECORD ACTUAL PROFIT → SCALE ONLY WINNERS.**

## Why the current store became unprofitable

### 1. Listing price was often set before full landed economics

Known examples:

- Weed wacker sold for **$49.98** against a known Doba source candidate of **$62.59** before eBay fees.
- Spotlight sold for **$34.98**, below the known **$35.90 MAP** and below both viable margin screens for the known source paths.
- Folding bed sold repeatedly around **$50.66–$55.66** while one actual fulfilled order landed at **$44.46** and the current known source structure still fails the 30% operating screen at those sale prices.
- Lawn sweeper sold for **$69.99** against a final Doba shipped total of **$63.36**.
- Historical boot-dryer fulfillment reached **$14.00** against a **$10.89** sale.

These are sales, but they are not healthy revenue.

### 2. eBay fees consume roughly another ~15–16% on normal current orders

Current authenticated Seller Hub transaction evidence shows typical transaction-fee rates around the mid-teens on the current order set; very low-ticket items can be worse because fixed fee components become proportionally larger.

Therefore the existing **30% pre-fee gross-margin screen is a true minimum, not a comfortable target**. A 30% pre-fee spread can leave only the mid-teens before other variable costs. The 35% preferred screen should be used for normal retained core listings whenever the market supports it.

### 3. Doba became a blanket catalog source instead of a product-by-product source

Doba is useful for some products and for recovering committed customer obligations, but broad feed-style use created listings whose source cost did not support eBay pricing.

The fix is not "remove Doba from everything." The fix is:

**PRODUCT → DEMAND → AUTHORIZED SOURCE → CURRENT LANDED COST → PROFITABLE PRICE → RELIABLE FULFILLMENT.**

Doba stays only where it wins that comparison.

### 4. Fulfillment verification happened too late

Current recovery shows late orders, missing tracking and unresolved supplier execution. This has created cancellations, customer friction and payout holds.

Every new order must be source-verified and placed immediately after payment rather than being discovered near the ship-by deadline.

### 5. Too many active listings increased operating complexity without proving profit

The owner-directed contraction to approximately **12 active revenue listings maximum** is correct for Phase 1. The store should earn the right to expand again through clean fulfillment, released payouts and positive actual contribution.

## Current cash-control reality

Authenticated Seller Hub reconciliation identified **8 held transactions totaling $242.79**.

That cash is not the profitability problem by itself, but it exposes the consequence of poor fulfillment discipline: revenue that cannot quickly become usable cash.

Priority is:

**VALID TRACKING → DELIVERY → HOLD RELEASE → RESOLVE LATE/FAILED ORDERS → STOP REPEATING THE SAME SOURCE/PRICE ERROR.**

## New retained-listing profit gate

A listing should remain active only when all of the following are true:

1. **Demand:** sale, watcher, meaningful views, or a strong verified demand case.
2. **Marketplace authority:** the exact source may be used for eBay.
3. **Exact product/source identity:** no silent source substitution.
4. **Current landed source cost:** product + supplier shipping/freight + any source-side variable cost.
5. **Price floor / MAP:** customer price is compliant.
6. **Pre-fee gross-margin screen:** **30% hard working minimum; 35%+ preferred.**
7. **Positive order contribution after eBay fees and all other variable costs.**
8. **Executable handling time:** source can reliably ship within the listing promise.
9. **Routing risk controlled:** AK/HI, lithium/dangerous goods, oversized freight and other special routes cannot create unsupported obligations.
10. **Actuals loop:** after every sale, actual landed cost and actual eBay fees are written back into the product decision.

### Operating formula

**EXPECTED CONTRIBUTION = ITEM REVENUE + BUYER-PAID SHIPPING − LANDED SOURCE COST − EBAY FEES − PROMOTED LISTING FEES − ELEVATION-FUNDED DISCOUNTS/CREDITS − OTHER VARIABLE ORDER COSTS**

Recommended operating posture during recovery:

- 35%+ pre-fee gross margin = normal preferred listing target;
- 30–35% = allowed only when fulfillment is reliable and actual contribution remains worthwhile;
- below 30% = do not scale; reprice, resource or end;
- negative expected contribution = do not list/fulfill as a normal commercial sale unless Casey explicitly approves a customer-recovery or strategic exception.

## Phase 0 — customer + cash recovery

Before growth work:

- resolve every unshipped late/cancel-requested order;
- never cancel a verified shipped order;
- attach/verify valid tracking immediately;
- let completed refunds unwind without duplicate refunds;
- monitor delivery and payout release;
- do not place uneconomic rescue orders merely to avoid admitting a failed listing.

The current held-funds incident should be substantially resolved or clearly aging toward scheduled release before catalog expansion.

## Phase 1 — contract to the core

Target: **approximately 12 active revenue listings maximum**.

Classify every active listing as:

- `CORE KEEP — APPROVED + PROFITABLE`
- `KEEP HISTORY / REPRICE`
- `END / REBUILD AFTER SOURCE APPROVAL`
- `END / DROP`
- `TEMP ORDER-PROTECTION HOLD`

End first:

- proven negative-margin listings;
- below-MAP / policy-risk listings;
- ordinary parcel lithium listings without safe route controls;
- zero/near-zero demand listings with no strategic source advantage;
- anything with unknown economics that is not protecting a current paid order.

Do not destroy demand history in Git. The historical data becomes the sourcing/rebuild lead list.

## Phase 2 — rebuild the core around actual demand

The first rebuild pool should come from products that have already shown demand, not from another supplier feed dump.

Priority candidates:

1. camping cot / folding bed — repeated sales; find a source/price combination that supports margin and fast shipping;
2. boot dryer — repeat sales/watchers; only relaunch at profitable cost/price;
3. organizer — current demand signal; clear exact cost immediately;
4. camping fan — verify exact SKU/source/economics;
5. lawn sweeper — demand proven but likely needs a materially better source to remain competitive;
6. carefully selected RV/outdoor utility products with simple domestic parcel fulfillment.

Do not force all 12 slots full. Six excellent listings are better than twelve marginal listings.

## Phase 3 — fulfillment becomes part of the product decision

For every paid order:

**PAYMENT → EXACT SKU → SOURCE RECHECK → COST RECHECK → CONTRIBUTION RECHECK → PLACE SUPPLIER ORDER → CAPTURE ACCEPTANCE → CAPTURE TRACKING → UPDATE EBAY → MONITOR DELIVERY → RECORD ACTUAL PROFIT.**

Internal target: supplier/order execution should begin the same operating session in which the paid order is discovered whenever the source is executable.

No order should reach its ship-by date with source identity or supplier-order state still unknown.

## Phase 4 — scale only verified winners

Expansion beyond the Phase 1 cap requires:

- no unmanaged late orders;
- tracking discipline proven;
- payout hold materially normalized;
- retained listings show positive actual contribution;
- supplier/source reliability is established;
- the next product batch passes the same gate before publication.

Scale by **product family and actual contribution**, not listing count.
