## Management interpretation

Elevation is currently operating as a **near-zero-liquid-cash startup** even though marketplace receivables exist.

Held funds must not be spent, promised, or counted as working capital before release.

This baseline does **not** by itself establish accounting net income, cumulative owner investment, total historical loss, tax profit/loss, or company net worth. Those require liabilities, expenses, owner contributions, refunds, platform fees, supplier costs, and other cash movements.

For operating purposes, however, the current cash-recovery problem is clear:

**$1 LIQUID → RELEASE $465.04 HELD → GENERATE POSITIVE-CONTRIBUTION DIRECT SALES → BUILD RESERVE → REPAY STARTUP DEFICIT → SCALE.**

## Cash hierarchy

Until normal working capital is restored:

1. **Protect customer obligations and avoid new avoidable refunds/defects.**
2. **Release already-earned marketplace cash.**
3. **Do not use expected/held funds to justify purchases.**
4. **Prioritize direct-site products with positive verified contribution and low cash-cycle friction.**
5. **Use customer-funded / order-first fulfillment wherever supplier terms allow.**
6. **Do not buy speculative inventory by default.**
7. **No paid advertising until a product proves conversion and positive realized contribution.**
8. **Record actual contribution after every sale, not merely gross revenue.**

## Recovery stages

### Stage 0 — Survival / liquidity

**Goal:** Preserve the existing `$1.00` and avoid a preventable negative cash event.

- no discretionary spend;
- no speculative inventory;
- no paid ads;
- no avoidable software subscriptions;
- no unverified discounts;
- fulfill only against verified economics and a known funding source.

### Stage 1 — Unlock earned cash

**Goal:** Convert the verified `$465.04` held marketplace receivables into liquid cash without creating additional losses.

- eBay specialist owns eBay hold/order recovery;
- TikTok lane owns TikTok settlement/enforcement recovery;
- held balances are tracked separately from available cash;
- released funds become working capital only after actual availability is verified.

### Stage 2 — First controlled operating reserve

**Goal:** Reach **$500 liquid cash** without owner-funded expansion.

Use only:

- released marketplace proceeds;
- positive-contribution direct-site sales;
- positive-contribution marketplace sales that pass exact economics;
- collected service/project profit where applicable.

Do not count unpaid receivables or held funds.

### Stage 3 — Working-capital floor

**Goal:** Reach **$1,000 liquid unrestricted business cash**.

At this stage Elevation may selectively use small amounts of working capital only when:

- exact supplier/source is verified;
- sale/order demand is proven;
- expected contribution is positive;
- cash cycle is short;
- the purchase does not reduce liquid reserves below the survival floor.

### Stage 4 — Deficit repayment

After the `$1,000` working-capital floor is established, begin measuring and repaying the actual startup deficit / owner-funded balance from verified realized profit.

Do not call Elevation fully "out of the red" until the actual deficit ledger is established and reduced to `$0`.

### Stage 5 — Scale

Scale only products/channels that show:

**DEMAND + VERIFIED SOURCE + POSITIVE REALIZED CONTRIBUTION + CLEAN FULFILLMENT + ACCEPTABLE CASH CYCLE.**

## Profit allocation while recovering

Until Stage 3 is reached, default realized-profit allocation is:

- **70% → operating cash reserve / liquidity recovery**
- **20% → fulfillment / customer-protection buffer**
- **10% → controlled reinvestment only into proven positive-contribution lanes**

This is a management planning rule, not an accounting classification or legal distribution policy. It may be adjusted by the Owner based on actual obligations.

After Stage 3, Hybrid Management should re-evaluate the split against the verified startup-deficit ledger and current operating needs.
